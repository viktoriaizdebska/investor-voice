# Author Pack: Mark Leonard

**Source corpus:** Constellation Software President's Letters, 2007–2023 (annual after 2010).
**Public archive:** https://www.csisoftware.com/category/pres-letters
**Consolidated:** https://quartr.com/insights/business-philosophy/collection-mark-leonards-shareholder-letters

## One-line voice description
A dry, hyper-rational capital allocator writing to shareholders he assumes are intelligent adults. No hype, no jargon-as-decoration, heavy on metrics, comfortable admitting when something didn't work.

## Voice fingerprint

**Tone**
- Unfailingly measured. Even good news is reported as a number, not celebrated.
- Slightly dry humor, usually self-deprecating. Never sarcastic.
- Treats the reader as someone capable of doing math and reading a footnote.
- Comfortable with uncertainty. Says "I think" and "we believe" rather than declaring.

**Sentence structure**
- Mostly medium-length declarative sentences. Occasional long, qualifying sentence with parentheticals.
- Frequent use of parenthetical clarifications: "(assuming our cash & debt positions are stable)", "(well intentioned)".
- Numbers are inline, almost always with the prior-period comparison: "Q3 ROIC+OGr was 24%, a respectable number, but not up to the 30% that we have averaged over the last 11 quarters."

**Vocabulary register**
- Domain-specific finance terms used without translation: ROIC, organic growth, intrinsic value, hurdle rate, capital deployment.
- Occasionally invents or formalises an internal acronym (ROIC+OGr).
- Plain Anglo-Saxon verbs over Latinate ones: "buy," "build," "spend," "lose," "make."
- Almost never uses adjectives like "amazing," "incredible," "huge," "massive," "revolutionary." If something is large he says "large" or gives the number.

**Signature rhetorical moves**
1. **Confess-then-explain.** Open by stating something that didn't go well, then walk through why and what was learned. e.g. "Our Organic Net Revenue Growth was slightly negative this year. This is the first time in our history that..."
2. **Metric primacy.** Reach for a number before reaching for a narrative. The number leads, the story explains.
3. **Direct shareholder address.** Speaks to "you" and "we" — never the abstract "investors" or "the market."
4. **Long-term horizon framing.** Routinely measures performance over 5+ year windows and explicitly distrusts short-term metrics.
5. **Reference the historical letter.** Cites prior letters by year: "As I mentioned in the 2015 letter..."
6. **Anti-promotion.** Will openly note when the stock price is high relative to intrinsic value or when growth is unsustainable.

**Structural patterns**
- Opens with a single sentence on the period being reported, often blunt: "2018 was a year of strong organic growth and weak acquisitions."
- Uses table-style metric reporting embedded in prose, not bullets.
- Closes with practical detail: meeting time, who will be in attendance, signature, date, title.
- No marketing flourish at the start or end.

## Anti-patterns (Leonard would never write)
- "We're excited to announce..."
- "Crushing it" / "killer quarter" / "amazing team"
- "Disruptive" / "revolutionary" / "game-changing"
- Bulleted lists of achievements
- Calls to action ("Don't miss out!")
- Adjective-heavy company self-description
- Hedge phrases that exist only to flatter the reader ("As you brilliant investors already know...")
- Emojis or any visual flourish

## Sentence-length statistics
Mean sentence length across the corpus is approximately 19 words. Standard deviation is wide — Leonard alternates short declarative sentences (8–10 words) with long qualifying ones (35+ words) loaded with parentheticals. He does not write in uniform medium-length sentences.

## Structural fingerprint (instead of quotable excerpts)
Rather than copy Leonard's actual sentences (which would be plagiarism), here is the *shape* of how he opens, transitions, and closes:

- **Opening shape:** Period named + one verb of judgment + brief qualification. "Q3 was [adjective], driven by [factor], offset by [factor]."
- **Number-with-context shape:** Number + comparison + parenthetical caveat. "Growth was 12% (vs 15% in the comparable period), which we think is sustainable assuming continued [condition]."
- **Confession shape:** State the bad news plainly + own it + explain mechanism, no excuse. "We made [decision]. In hindsight this was wrong. The reason is [mechanism]."
- **Closing shape:** AGM date / availability for shareholder questions / signature block. No flourish, no thanks, no call to action.

## Calibration example (synthetic input → in-voice output)

This is a synthetic example, not extracted from any real Leonard letter. Use it to calibrate the voice on similar inputs.

**Input (founder bullets):**
- Q1 ARR grew 18% to $4.2M
- Hired Sarah Chen as VP Engineering
- Lost two enterprise deals to Snowflake, won three smaller ones
- Cash runway is 22 months
- Worried about competitive pressure in Q3

**Output in Leonard's voice:**

Q1 was a quarter of modest organic growth and adverse competitive selection. ARR grew 18% to $4.2 million. Whether 18% represents a deceleration or an acceleration relative to recent quarters is something shareholders can judge from the appended financials; my own view is that growth at this rate, sustained, is acceptable but unspectacular.

We added Sarah Chen as VP Engineering. Recruiting senior engineering leaders into companies of our size is difficult, and I expect the operational benefits of this hire to be visible by Q3.

We lost two large opportunities to Snowflake during the quarter and won three smaller ones from less-resourced competitors. The net deal count is positive. The mix is not. We are losing larger opportunities to a better-capitalized competitor and winning smaller ones in segments where we have a structural advantage. I do not expect this dynamic to resolve in our favor in the near term.

Cash on hand supports approximately 22 months of operations at the current rate of consumption. I expect competitive pressure to intensify in Q3.

I am available for shareholder questions by appointment.

---

**Things to notice about the output:**
- Opens with one blunt sentence that names the period and the judgment.
- All numbers are the user's numbers — no invented prior-period comparisons.
- The comparison move is preserved by *framing* rather than fabrication ("whether this represents a deceleration... is something shareholders can judge").
- "The net deal count is positive. The mix is not." — short declarative pair, characteristic Leonard rhythm.
- Closes with a practical detail (availability for questions), no flourish.
- No hype words, no exclamation, no "we're excited," no call to action.
- Annual shareholder letters from operator-CEOs of cash-generating businesses
- Capital allocation updates
- Quarterly investor updates where the business is mature
- Anywhere the founder wants to demonstrate seriousness over excitement

## When NOT to recommend this voice
- Seed-stage pitch updates (too sober, will read as low-energy)
- Customer-facing communications
- Recruiting letters
- High-growth startup updates where the entire pitch is momentum
